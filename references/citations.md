## Cross-cutting (all skills)

**Knowles, M. — Andragogy (The Adult Learner, 1973; 3rd ed. 1984)** · PAID (book), PARTIAL (overviews)
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andragogy
- https://instructionaldesign.org/?p=98
- Use for: adults need the *why*, want immediate relevance, and learn problem-first rather than content-first. Includes Knowles's own worked example of applying andragogy to computer training.
- Caveat: Wikipedia notes there is no single agreed theory of adult learning.

**Dakan, R., Feller, J., & Anthropic — AI Fluency Framework (4D), 2025** · FREE (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)
- https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/b383cf6baddbfc72fdf8b0ed533a518e2872d531.pdf
- https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/334975cdec18f744b4fa511dc8518bd8d119d29d.pdf
- Course: https://www.coursera.org/learn/ai-fluency-framework-foundations/reviews
- Use for: the competency vocabulary for what the client must become fluent in (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence), and its two loops (Delegation–Diligence, Description–Discernment).

## 1. break-down-course-goal

- UbD white paper (ASCD 2014, hosted by McTighe): https://jaymctighe.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/UbD-White-Paper.pdf
- Chapter 1, "What Is Backward Design?": https://digitallearning.newhaven.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Understanding-by-Design.pdf
- Use for: the three stages (results → evidence → plan), transfer goals, and backward design as the alternative to coverage-driven and activity-driven plans.

**van Merriënboer, J. & Kirschner, P. — Ten Steps to Complex Learning / 4C/ID** · PAID (4th ed. 2024/25), FREE (2nd ed. listed open access)
- Open University research portal (2nd ed. open-access file): https://research.ou.nl/en/publications/%E7%BB%BC%E5%90%88%E5%AD%A6%E4%B9%A0%E7%9A%84%E5%8D%81%E4%B8%AA%E6%AD%A5%E9%AA%A4/
- 4C/ID chapter abstract: https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/4cid-in-the-context-of-instructional-design-and-the-learning-scie/

## 3. assign-topics-to-sessions

**Reigeluth, C. — Elaboration Theory; Simplifying Conditions Method** · FREE (chapter scan, summaries)
- Reigeluth & Stein chapter (METU OCW): https://ocw.metu.edu.tr/mod/resource/view.php?id=4412
- Summary: https://tecfa.unige.ch/themes/sa2/act-app-dos2-fic-elaborat.htm
- Use for: simple-to-complex sequencing starting from an "epitome," the simplest whole version. The Simplifying Conditions Method suggests opening the course with a toy version of the capstone simulation.

**Bjork, E. & Bjork, R. — Desirable difficulties: spacing, interleaving, testing** · FREE
- "Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way" (2011): https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf

**Spolsky, J. — "Evidence Based Scheduling" (2007)** · FREE
- https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2007/10/26/evidence-based-scheduling/
- Use for: calibrating estimates against measured history. Applied here as a per-mode correction factor from session-review timestamps, with probability ranges instead of a single number.

## 4. design-lab

**Resnick, M. — Lifelong Kindergarten; low floor / high ceiling / wide walls** · PAID (book), FREE (papers)
- "Scratch: Programming for Everyone" (CACM): https://web.media.mit.edu/~mres/scratch/scratch-cacm.pdf
- "Designing for Wide Walls" *(linked from a result, not opened)*: https://mres.medium.com/designing-for-wide-walls-323bdb4e7277
- Use for: designing at-home play, with an easy start, room to grow, and many possible directions.

**Kapur, M. — Productive Failure** · FREE (articles), PAID (2024 book)
- Kapur in Times Higher Education: https://www.timeshighereducation.com/campus/using-productive-failure-activate-deeper-learning
- Summary of the 53-study review: https://evidencebasedliving.human.cornell.edu/blog/to-better-learn-students-must-first-fail
- Use for: planted errors, and running a lab *before* its lecture for some topics. The design must be challenging but not impossible, and it needs some prior knowledge.

**Ericsson, K. A., Krampe, R., & Tesch-Römer, C. (1993) — Deliberate practice** · FREE (replication paper), PARTIAL (original)
- Replication: Macnamara & Maitra (2019), *Royal Society Open Science*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6731745/
- Original, record page: https://stafforini.com/works/ericsson-1993-role-deliberate-practice/
- Use for: focused repetitions with feedback (the "change one thing" drill).
- **Caveat:** the replication found a smaller effect and inconsistent definitions. Cite the principle, not the "10,000 hours."

**Mollick, E. & Mollick, L. — "Assigning AI: Seven Approaches for Students, with Prompts" (2023)** · FREE
- https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10052
- https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4475995
- Use for: AI roles in practice (tutor, coach, simulator, student, and others), keeping the human in the loop, and concrete prompt patterns for labs and homework.

## 8. review-feedback

**Kirkpatrick — Four Levels (reaction, learning, behavior, results)** · PARTIAL (official overview)
- https://kirkpatrickpartners.com/the-kirkpatrick-model/
- Use for: measuring more than coverage in reviews (behavior = is the client using it between sessions?). The official guidance to start from Level 4, results, matches backward design.

**Hattie, J. & Timperley, H. — "The Power of Feedback" (2007)** · FREE
- https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/575/Hattie_Timperly_2007.pdf
- Use for: structuring reviews as feed up (where are we going), feed back (how did it go), and feed forward (where next), with feed forward as the most powerful.

**US Army — After Action Review (TC 25-20, 1993; updated guides)** · FREE
- Civilian AAR guide with templates (National Comprehensive Center, 2020): https://www.adi.org/downloads/After-Action-Review-Guide.pdf
- Army Research Institute report: https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA463410.pdf
- Use for: the four-question debrief (intended, happened, why, change) without assigning blame. A planned-vs-actual log feeds time calibration.

## Findings from verification that affect the skill drafts

- **Lab before lecture, sometimes** (Kapur): productive failure reverses the usual order. design-lab and generate-curriculum should allow a lab to run first when the student has enough background to try.
- **Play needs a seed** (Victor; Resnick): fully open sandboxes underperform. At-home play gets a starting prompt and room to wander.
- **Open with a toy capstone** (Reigeluth's Simplifying Conditions Method): show the simplest whole version of the course's capstone early, then elaborate session by session.
- **The deliberate-practice effect is smaller than its reputation** (2019 replication): keep the drill, drop strong claims.
